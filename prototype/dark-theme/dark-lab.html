Five dark grounds, measured

The light theme was chosen from four prototyped directions. The dark theme was never chosen at all — its ground and its gradient are the values that happened to be in the file before the token refactor. This page puts five candidate dark grounds through the same harness the light palettes were judged in: the real portfolio, real copy, live gradients, and every ratio computed against the actual composited pixel rather than a flat swatch. Pick a plate, or steal across them.

01

Dark is undesigned, not chosen

During the light rework, dark was held fixed on purpose: light was designed against it, so redesigning both at once would have left nothing to judge by. That was the right call then, and it is why this question was deferred rather than answered.

What it leaves behind is an asymmetry. Checking the pre-refactor file (ef969ed) against today: dark’s ground is still 0 0% 15% and its four blobs are still 80% 60% — unchanged, to the digit. Only the accent moved (45° amber → 160° green) and the names. Light, meanwhile, got four candidate directions and a deliberate pick: a tinted 230 32% 96% ground and a gradient desaturated into the 40s because AA forced it.

So the two themes differ in kind, not just value. Light has an identity someone selected. Dark has an untinted default grey and the loudest gradient in the codebase, and nobody ever said yes to either.

02

The ground is cosmetic; the gradient is the lever

Before picking a direction, it is worth knowing which knob does the work. Holding everything else at today’s values and moving one thing at a time:

  • Tint the ground alone — body text on the page goes 3.28:1 → 3.45:1. That is noise.
  • Calm the gradient alone — the same pairing goes 3.28:1 → 5.22:1, crossing from “passes because it is large text” to clearing the full AA body floor.

This is the light theme’s rule turning up again in the other theme. CONTEXT.md already records that the gradient field is the binding accessibility constraint, not the palette — discovered while fixing light. It holds here for the same reason: at peak coverage a blob is the background under the text, and the ground barely participates.

The consequence for this decision: a candidate that only tints the ground is a cosmetic change, and a candidate that calms the gradient is an accessibility change that happens to also look different.

03

How the ratios are measured

Every number on this page is computed in the browser, from the same token values the plate is rendered with — nothing is transcribed by hand.

Raised surfaces are translucent over a moving gradient, so a pairing is only as good as its worst moment. Each ratio is therefore the minimum across five backdrops: the bare ground, plus each of the four blobs composited at 0.55 peak coverage. Where the foreground itself carries alpha, it is composited too. A flat swatch would flatter every one of these numbers, which is exactly the mistake CONTEXT.md exists to prevent.

One row is expected to fail in four of the five plates: accent on page. Putting highlight-coloured text directly on the page ground measures 2.05:1 in today’s dark theme, and no lightness value fixes it — the accent is squeezed between a dark ground and bright blobs. Nothing in the app does this, and the rule against it is documented. Candidate C is the one plate where that constraint lifts.

09

Where the page meets the hero

The hero is an inverted region: it stays dark in both themes and re-declares its own tokens, with a ground of 240 6% 4%. In the light theme that produces a hard edge, which is issue #3. In dark the same edge exists but is quiet, because the page is already dark.

How quiet depends on the ground you pick, so it is worth seeing. Below, each candidate is rendered with the hero sitting directly above the page — the seam is the join.

10

What happens after you pick

The token refactor already did the expensive part. A dark direction is four to ten values inside the :root block in src/index.css — no component changes, because nothing hard-codes a colour any more.

Two things to decide alongside the plate, not after it. Candidate C changes what raised means in dark, from a darkening to a lift, which is a vocabulary change and wants a line in CONTEXT.md. And if the gradient calms, say so in the rule: the existing note about gradient lightness is written as a light-theme constraint, and it would become a rule about both themes.

D is the answer to “unify the hero but keep the gradient”: same animation as today, no seam, and every ratio up. What it gives up relative to C is only what C bought with its dimmer gradient.

Mixing is fine and probably likely — B’s gradient on REF’s grey ground, or C’s seam with B’s brightness. Say which parts and the values get derived and re-measured before anything lands.
